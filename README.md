## 🧠 What Is Borehole Drilling?

Borehole drilling involves creating a deep, narrow opening into the ground to access groundwater stored within underground geological formations.

Once groundwater is reached, specialised equipment can be installed to bring the water to the surface.

The water can potentially be used for residential, commercial, agricultural, irrigation, and other suitable applications, depending on the water quality and the requirements of the property.

The important thing to remember is that every property is different.

The depth of a borehole, the geological conditions, the available water yield, and the equipment required can vary significantly.

That is why a professional assessment is an important starting point.

---

## 🔎 Step 1: Start With a Geo Survey and Site Assessment

A successful borehole project starts before the drilling rig arrives.

A geo survey and site assessment can help identify a suitable drilling location by considering geological conditions and other relevant site characteristics.

Choosing an appropriate location can help reduce unnecessary drilling risks and improve the planning process.

The property's intended water usage should also be considered.

A family home, commercial property, and agricultural operation may have very different water requirements.

The borehole should therefore be planned around the property and its expected usage.

---

## 🚧 Step 2: Prepare the Property

Once the drilling location has been selected, the site needs to be prepared.

The drilling team may need to consider:

✔ Access for the drilling equipment

✔ Available working space

✔ Potential underground services or obstacles

✔ Safe working conditions

✔ Ground and site conditions

Proper preparation helps the drilling operation proceed as efficiently and safely as possible.

---

## 🛠️ Step 3: Drill the Borehole

This is the part most people associate with borehole installation.

A specialised drilling rig is used to penetrate layers of soil and rock.

The drilling process can vary depending on the geological conditions encountered at the property.

During drilling, the team may monitor:

✔ Soil and rock formations

✔ Drilling depth

✔ Water strikes

✔ Water flow

✔ Borehole stability

Some properties may reach groundwater relatively quickly, while others may require deeper drilling.

This is one reason why **borehole drilling prices** can vary from one property to another.

---

## 💧 Step 4: Develop and Clean the Borehole

Drilling can leave sediment, drilling debris, and fine particles inside the borehole.

The borehole therefore needs to be properly cleaned and developed after drilling.

This process helps improve water flow and prepares the borehole for testing and equipment installation.

A properly developed borehole is an important foundation for the rest of the water system.

---

## 📊 Step 5: Test the Borehole

Finding water is only part of the process.

The borehole also needs to be assessed to determine how it performs.

Pump testing can provide information about:

✔ Water yield

✔ Sustainable pumping rate

✔ Recovery rate

✔ Overall borehole performance

These results help determine which pump and system configuration are appropriate.

This is important because selecting a pump should not simply be based on price.

The pump needs to be suitable for the borehole and the property's water requirements.

## 💦 Step 8: Don't Assume Borehole Water Is Automatically Drinking Water

One of the most important things for new borehole owners to understand is that groundwater quality can vary.

Just because water comes from a borehole does not automatically mean that it is suitable for drinking.

Water quality can be influenced by geological conditions and naturally occurring minerals.

Depending on the water quality and intended use, purification may be required.

LiquaFlo provides tailor-made purification systems designed around the customer's specific water requirements.

Depending on the situation, purification equipment may include:

✔ Sediment filtration

✔ Carbon filtration

✔ UV treatment

✔ Reverse osmosis

✔ Iron and manganese treatment

The appropriate solution should be determined according to the characteristics of the water rather than simply installing unnecessary equipment.

## 🚨 Don't Choose a Borehole System Based on Price Alone

The cheapest quote is not automatically the most suitable solution.

A borehole system is an investment in your property's water infrastructure.

Before proceeding, consider whether the solution includes the equipment and services your property actually requires.

Ask questions such as:

✔ Has the site been properly assessed?

✔ How will the borehole be tested?

✔ How will the pump be selected?

✔ Is water purification required?

✔ What storage capacity is appropriate?

✔ What maintenance will be required?

✔ Are the supplied products SABS approved?

✔ What warranties are provided?

✔ Who will assist if the system needs repairs or upgrades?

Understanding what you are paying for can be just as important as comparing the final price.

## 🏡 Residential, Commercial and Agricultural Borehole Solutions

Different properties require different water solutions.

### Residential Boreholes

Homeowners may use borehole water for applications such as gardening, irrigation, household supply, or other suitable uses depending on water quality and treatment requirements.

A residential system may include a borehole pump, storage tank, pressure equipment, and purification.

### Commercial Boreholes

Commercial properties may have significantly higher water demands.

Their systems may require larger pumps, increased storage capacity, pressure management, purification, and more extensive control equipment.

### Agricultural Boreholes

Agricultural properties can have substantial water requirements for irrigation, livestock, farming operations, and other applications.

The borehole yield, pump capacity, storage requirements, and distribution system all need to be considered when designing the solution.
